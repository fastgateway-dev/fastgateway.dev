# Live Demo

Try FastGateway right now in a free, hosted sandbox — no cluster, no install, no sign-up.

:::tip Open the sandbox

**[demo.fastgateway.dev](https://demo.fastgateway.dev)** &nbsp;·&nbsp; username `admin` &nbsp;·&nbsp; password `admin`

The demo runs in an **isolated environment that is wiped and rebuilt every 30 minutes**, so feel free to break things — a fresh instance is never far away. Because it resets, treat anything you create as throwaway.

:::

This walkthrough takes you from an empty instance to a working domain and route. The steps build on each other, so follow them in order. The whole flow takes about ten minutes.

<Step number="1" title="Onboard your first project (in-cluster)" src="/img/demo-1-project.jpg" alt="Create an in-cluster project">

A **project** is where FastGateway provisions its Gateway, domains, and routes. The demo targets the cluster FastGateway itself runs in, so you'll create an **in-cluster** project.

1. Open **Projects** in the sidebar and click **New Project**.
2. Enter a **Project Name** (e.g. `demo`) and an optional description.
3. Under **Connection Type**, pick **In-Cluster (Local)** — FastGateway connects to the cluster it runs in using its own service account, so there's nothing else to configure. (The other options, *Kubeconfig* and *API URL + Token*, connect to a remote cluster.)
4. Click **Create Project**.

</Step>

<Step number="2" title="Create a team and a user" src="/img/demo-2-team-user.jpg" alt="Create a team and user" flip>

FastGateway is multi-tenant: **teams** own access to projects, and **users** belong to teams. As the admin, set up one of each (under *Admin → IAM* in the sidebar).

1. Open **Teams** and click **Create Team** — give it a name (e.g. `platform`) and an optional description.
2. Open **Users** and add a user with a username and password.
3. Add that user to the `platform` team.

</Step>

<Step number="3" title="Create a self-signed issuer and assign it to the project" src="/img/demo-3-issuer.jpg" alt="Create a self-signed certificate issuer">

Certificates are signed by an **issuer** (backed by cert-manager). For the demo, a **self-signed CA** is enough to get TLS working end to end without an external CA.

1. Open **Issuers** (under *Certificate*) and click **New Issuer**.
2. Keep the **Issuer Type** as **Self-signed CA**, then set a **Name** (e.g. `selfsigned`) and a **Common Name** (e.g. `demo-ca`). Leave the RSA / 2048 / 3650-day defaults and click **Create Issuer**.
3. On the issuer's page, under **Grant Project Access**, select your `demo` project and click **Grant** — now the project is allowed to issue certificates from this issuer.

</Step>

<Step number="4" title="Onboard the team to the project" src="/img/demo-4-onboard-team.jpg" alt="Grant the team access to the project" flip>

Projects aren't visible to a team until they're granted access. Onboard the `platform` team to the `demo` project so its members can manage domains and routes.

1. Open your `demo` project and go to its **Teams** tab (**Project Teams**).
2. Click **Assign Team** and select `platform`.
3. Pick a **Permission Preset** — e.g. **Editor**, which can create and edit domains, routes, clients, and certificates — then click **Assign Team**.

</Step>

<Step number="5" title="Create a domain template" src="/img/demo-5-template.jpg" alt="Create a domain template">

A **domain template** captures the reusable infrastructure settings every domain shares — controller, service type, TLS, autoscaling, and compute resources — so you configure them once.

1. In your `demo` project, open **Domain Templates** and click **Create Template**.
2. Give it a **Name** (e.g. `default`).
3. Pick a **Controller** (Envoy Gateway), a **Service Type** (LoadBalancer), and a **TLS** mode (TLS Only). Autoscaling, observability, and compute resources live under *Advanced Settings*.
4. Click **Create Template**.

</Step>

<Step number="6" title="Set up the TLS certificate" src="/img/demo-6-certificate.jpg" alt="The All Certificates page, where managed certificates are tracked" flip>

FastGateway terminates HTTPS with a Kubernetes **TLS secret** that's signed by the self-signed issuer you granted in step 3 — there's no certificate file to upload by hand. Every managed certificate across your projects is listed under **All Certificates**.

1. Open **All Certificates** (under *Certificate*) to see where issued certificates appear. It's empty on a fresh instance and fills in as HTTPS domains come online.
2. Decide on the **secret name** your domain will use (e.g. `demo-tls`) — you'll point the domain at it in the next step.
3. Because the `selfsigned` issuer is granted to your project, FastGateway can sign this certificate for you, no external CA required.

</Step>

<Step number="7" title="Create a domain" src="/img/demo-7-domain.jpg" alt="Create a domain">

A **domain** ties a hostname to a template and a TLS secret, and provisions the Gateway listener.

1. In your `demo` project open **Domains** and click **New Domain**.
2. Choose the **Domain Template** `default` — its settings (LoadBalancer, TLS Only, port 443) are shown for confirmation.
3. Enter a **Display Name** and the **Hostname** (e.g. `demo.example.com`).
4. Set the **TLS Secret Name** (e.g. `demo-tls`) — the certificate from step 6. You can also tick *Automatically create the DNS record* once you've registered a hosted zone.
5. Click **Create Domain** — FastGateway provisions the Gateway listener for your hostname.

</Step>

<Step number="8" title="Create a route" src="/img/demo-8-route.jpg" alt="Create a route" flip>

Finally, add a **route** that forwards matching traffic on your domain to a backend.

1. Open your domain and click **New Route**.
2. On **Basic Info**, give the route a **name** (e.g. `demo-route`).
3. On **Traffic**, keep **Forward to Backend** and add a **backend** — a Kubernetes Service (namespace, service, port) or an external service. Add more backends to split traffic by weight.
4. The **Extensions** and **Security** tabs add header rewrites, redirects, CORS, API keys, JWT, and more — all optional.
5. Click **Create**. The change runs through the project's approval workflow, then FastGateway renders the `HTTPRoute` and your domain is serving traffic.

:::note
You create routes as a member of a team assigned to the project — that's why step 4 onboarded the `platform` team.
:::

</Step>

## What you just built

You now have a complete path from request to backend: an in-cluster **project**, a **team** with access, a self-signed **issuer**, a **domain template**, a **TLS secret**, a **domain**, and a **route**. This is the same flow you'd follow on a real cluster — see [Getting Started](/docs/getting-started/installation) to run FastGateway yourself, or the [Concepts](/docs/concepts/projects) for how each piece fits together.
